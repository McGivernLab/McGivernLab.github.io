## Edinburgh 2026: Investigating Cognition at Microscales: Conceptual and Empirical Challenges

This workshop aims to examine the conceptual and experimental challenges involved in investigating cognition at microscales, with particular focus on putative learning, memory, and decision-making capacities in unicellular and non-neuronal organisms such as Stentor coeruleus and Amoeba proteus. While cognition and learning have been extensively studied in macroscale organisms, experimental paradigms and theoretical frameworks in comparative cognition have largely been developed around animals inhabiting macroscale environments. Whether these paradigms can be straightforwardly applied to microorganisms remains unresolved. The goal of this workshop is to clarify the particular challenges of investigating cognition at microscales, both in terms of the conceptual challenges relating to how cognitive capacities are described and identified, and the empirical and methodological challenges concerning experimental design, behavioural operationalisation, ecological validity, and standards of evidence appropriate for investigating microscope cognition. It brings together philosophers, cognitive scientists, experimental biologists and biophysicists to investigate how physical scale, environmental structure, and organismal life history constrain the study of cognition and learning, while clarifying which conceptual and methodological standards should guide future research in microscale cognition.
